Student Investigation

Use the app as a reader first. Write your answers in the separate practice notebook. Run Streamlit apps in a terminal, not as notebook cells.

Day 1: a useful interface

  1. Describe a specific person and a question they would ask about your final-project data.
  2. Explain why repeated interaction would help. Could a figure or report meet the need?
  3. Choose one control. State its default, what it changes, and how the app should handle no matching results.
  4. Propose one useful summary metric. Define its denominator and explain which filters affect it.

Day 1: predict execution

In day1/02_execution.py, predict the first counter after two button clicks and after editing the name. Compare it to the Session State counter. Explain where the terminal output appears.

Day 2: explore the messages

  1. Choose a question about the represented messages, years, or recorded speaker roles.
  2. Find at least three kickers that help investigate it.
  3. Read a message that complicates your first interpretation.
  4. Open a source talk when the kicker is insufficient.
  5. State one supported finding and one next check. Restrict conclusions to this selected sample.

Day 2: reason about computation

For each change, decide whether loading, embedding generation, PHATE, filtering, and summary calculations need new results:

  • Change the point colors.
  • Hide a year from the existing map.
  • Edit one kicker’s text in the dataset.
  • Change the PHATE random-state argument.

Differentiate what the script executes from what a cache can reuse.

Optional extension

Improve one part of the app for your chosen user. Ask a partner to complete the task without coaching. Record the point where they hesitate and revise the interface.
